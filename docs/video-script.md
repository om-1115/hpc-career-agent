# Explainer video — narration

The voiceover of [HPC-Career-Demo.mp4](HPC-Career-Demo.mp4) (2:43, 1080p, captioned). Voice: “Aman”, the English (India) system voice on macOS.

**0:00 · intro** — Every student deserves a career conversation. This is HPC Career: a Holistic Progress Card that becomes every student's career passbook.

**0:08 · problem** — India has about twenty-four point eight crore school students. In one survey, ninety-three percent knew only seven career options, out of about two hundred and fifty. And fewer than one in ten low-income students in Classes nine to twelve had ever received career guidance.

**0:23 · gap** — The stream decision comes at fifteen, and it is mostly made on advice from home. The new Holistic Progress Card describes the whole child, but it stops short of the question every Class ten student faces: what next?

**0:35 · solution** — HPC Career adds a Career tab to the progress card: a ten-minute test, a report written for the student, real careers with real routes, and a counsellor to ask.

**0:44 · passbook** — Think of it as a passbook for a student's career. Just as a bank passbook records every deposit, HPC Career records every step: each test, each plan, each reflection, from Class nine to twelve, so students can look back and decide with evidence, not guesswork.

**1:00 · psychology** — The test rests on career psychology. Holland's RIASEC model sorts interests into six types: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising and Conventional. A student's top three become their code. Personality uses the Big Five, through the public-domain Mini-IPIP.

**1:17 · demo** — Here is Asha. She signs in with the code her school gave her, opens the Career tab, and starts the test. Fifty-six quick questions, one per screen: first what she would enjoy doing, then how she usually is. When she finishes, the server scores her answers, and her report is ready.

**1:47 · report** — Asha is a Thinker and a Helper. Her report explains what that means, shows her personality as strengths, and lists six careers that fit, each with the route from Class ten. It prints, for her family to talk through.

**1:59 · guide** — Then it guides, step by step: discover your interests, understand them in plain words, explore thirty-six careers ranked by fit, make a plan, talk it through with family and teachers, and come back each year to reflect.

**2:11 · teacher** — Her teacher sees the whole class: who has finished, where their interests lie, and each student's code. A starting point for every one-to-one and parent meeting.

**2:20 · honest** — It is open source, and honest about its limits: the report is a starting point, not a verdict, and only the student and their teacher see a result.

**2:28 · next** — Already built: an AI counsellor agent, made and tested in AgentFoundry. Next, a pilot with a partner school, then Hindi and more Indian languages.

**2:38 · close** — Every student deserves a career conversation. HPC Career puts one in their pocket.

Sources for the figures: UDISE+ 2023-24 (Ministry of Education); Mindler survey of 10,000 students aged 14–21 (2019); Bharat Career Aspiration Report 2024 (UNICEF YuWaah & iDreamCareer, 4,968 Class 9–12 students from low-income families in 25 states).
